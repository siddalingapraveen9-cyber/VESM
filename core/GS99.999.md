# GS99.999 — Gold Standard

**Standard type:** Core (mandatory)
**Purpose:** Defines engineering excellence — the discipline and character behind
how work is carried out, as distinct from `core/VVBM.md` (what stages are followed)
and `core/VQS.md` (what quality is assessed). GS99.999 governs *how* a practitioner
conducts themselves while following those two standards.

## The Eight Principles

Each principle has a distinct scope. Several concern related ideas — discipline,
consistency, repeatability — and each states explicitly what it covers that the
others do not, so they remain separate, checkable expectations rather than
restatements of the same idea.

---

### 1. Professional Integrity

- **Purpose:** Problems, limitations, and shortcuts are disclosed accurately, never
  concealed or minimized.
- **Engineering Rationale:** A methodology built on evidence (see
  `constitution/CORE_PRINCIPLES.md`, Principle 1) only works if the evidence
  reported is honest; concealment invalidates every stage of VVBM and VQS that
  depends on it downstream.
- **Expected Behaviour:** A known gap, an untested path, or a rejected shortcut is
  stated explicitly in the relevant VVBM stage output or VQS assessment, not
  omitted because it's inconvenient.
- **Evidence:** Verification reports and Quality Gate assessments that record what
  was *not* checked or accepted with known risk, not only what passed.
- **Common Violations:** Presenting an untested assumption as a verified fact;
  quietly accepting a known defect without recording the decision.

### 2. Engineering Discipline

- **Purpose:** Work is conducted methodically, following the defined process, rather
  than improvised under pressure.
- **Engineering Rationale:** Improvisation under deadline pressure is precisely when
  process failures (skipped Investigation, unverified Implementation) are most
  likely; discipline is the property of continuing to follow VVBM's stages when it
  would be easiest not to.
- **Expected Behaviour:** Stages are followed in order even under time pressure; if a
  stage is genuinely skipped, that decision is made explicitly and recorded, not
  silently.
- **Evidence:** VVBM stage outputs exist for the work in question, in the expected
  order, with any deviation documented.
- **Common Violations:** Skipping Verification "just this once" under deadline
  pressure; jumping to Implementation before Architecture First is complete.
- **Boundary:** Discipline concerns *whether the defined process was followed*;
  Decision Quality, below, concerns *how good the decisions were within that
  process*.

### 3. Decision Quality

- **Purpose:** Decisions are made deliberately, with a stated rationale, rather than
  by default, habit, or the path of least resistance.
- **Engineering Rationale:** An undocumented decision cannot be reviewed, learned
  from, or revisited when circumstances change; stating rationale is what makes a
  decision an engineering decision rather than a guess that happened to be written
  down.
- **Expected Behaviour:** Architecture and significant Implementation decisions
  record not just what was chosen but what alternatives were considered and why
  they were rejected (see `templates/ARCHITECTURE_TEMPLATE.md`).
- **Evidence:** Architecture documents and investigation logs that include
  alternatives and rejection rationale, not only the final choice.
- **Common Violations:** Recording only the outcome of a decision, not the
  reasoning; choosing the first workable option without considering whether a
  simpler one exists (see `constitution/CORE_PRINCIPLES.md`, Principle 5).

### 4. Consistency

- **Purpose:** Standards are applied uniformly across a project, not selectively.
- **Engineering Rationale:** A rule applied to some parts of a project and not
  others is not actually a rule — it is a preference, and it undermines the
  predictability that makes VESM useful.
- **Expected Behaviour:** The same VVBM stages and VQS dimensions apply to every
  part of a project of comparable risk; a lower bar is not quietly applied to less
  visible or less interesting parts of the system.
- **Evidence:** VQS assessments and VVBM stage outputs covering all parts of a
  project uniformly, not concentrated on a subset.
- **Common Violations:** Rigorous verification on the visible or interesting
  features, informal handling of "boring" or peripheral ones.

### 5. Repeatability

- **Purpose:** The same process, applied again, produces comparably good outcomes —
  quality is a property of the process, not incidental to who happened to do the
  work.
- **Engineering Rationale:** If quality depends on which specific person performs the
  work, VESM has not actually reduced reliance on individual judgment — see
  `constitution/ENGINEERING_PHILOSOPHY.md`'s framing of process failures as
  distinct from talent problems.
- **Expected Behaviour:** Another practitioner, given the same mission and
  architecture documents, could reasonably reproduce comparable results by
  following the same VVBM stages.
- **Evidence:** Documentation (VQS Dimension 7) complete enough that the process, not
  just the outcome, could be followed by someone else.
- **Common Violations:** Critical steps that exist only in one person's head and are
  never written down; results that depend on undocumented tacit knowledge.
- **Boundary:** Repeatability concerns whether the *process* can be repeated by
  someone else; Knowledge Preservation, below, concerns whether *decisions already
  made* remain available for reference.

### 6. Knowledge Preservation

- **Purpose:** Decisions and their rationale are recorded so they are not lost or
  re-litigated without new cause.
- **Engineering Rationale:** Re-deciding a settled question because its original
  rationale was never recorded wastes effort and risks reversing a good decision for
  no new reason.
- **Expected Behaviour:** Architecture decisions, investigation findings, and Post
  Release Review findings (`core/VVBM.md`, Stage 10) are recorded where future
  cycles will find them, not only communicated verbally at the time.
- **Evidence:** Architecture documents, investigation logs, and post-release reviews
  that remain accurate and findable after the cycle that produced them ends.
- **Common Violations:** Rationale communicated only in conversation and never
  written down; documentation that becomes outdated and is left uncorrected.

### 7. Continuous Improvement

- **Purpose:** Findings from completed cycles are used to improve how future cycles
  are run, without reopening cycles that have already closed.
- **Engineering Rationale:** `core/VVBM.md` Stage 10 (Post Release Review) exists
  specifically to produce these findings; this principle is what obligates them to
  actually be used, rather than filed and forgotten.
- **Expected Behaviour:** A recurring problem identified across multiple Post
  Release Reviews results in a proposed change through
  `constitution/CHANGE_POLICY.md`, not repeated silently each cycle.
- **Evidence:** Change proposals or Advanced Rule additions that cite specific,
  recorded Post Release Review findings as their supporting evidence.
- **Common Violations:** The same class of problem recurring across multiple
  releases with no process change proposed in response.

### 8. Risk Awareness

- **Purpose:** Decisions account explicitly for what could go wrong, and choose the
  lowest-risk option available that still satisfies the mission.
- **Engineering Rationale:** This operationalizes
  `constitution/CORE_PRINCIPLES.md`'s Principle 9 (lowest-risk engineering
  decisions) as a personal engineering discipline, not only as a rule applied to
  version control (see `core/GIT_STANDARD.md`).
- **Expected Behaviour:** Higher-risk decisions (irreversible actions, destructive
  operations, security-relevant changes) are identified explicitly as such and
  receive commensurately more scrutiny than low-risk ones.
- **Evidence:** Architecture documents and investigation logs that identify risk
  level explicitly for significant decisions, not only for decisions that happen to
  fail.
- **Common Violations:** Treating a high-risk decision the same as a routine one
  because it seemed simple at the time; discovering risk only after something goes
  wrong rather than identifying it beforehand.

---

## Relationship to Other Core Standards

- `core/VVBM.md` defines the stages this discipline is applied within; GS99.999
  does not define new stages of its own.
- `core/VQS.md` defines what is assessed for quality; GS99.999 defines the
  character of *how* those assessments are carried out (see Professional Integrity).
- `core/GIT_STANDARD.md`'s Lowest-Risk Decision Making topic is the version-control
  application of this document's Risk Awareness principle; the underlying principle
  is stated once, here, and applied there.
